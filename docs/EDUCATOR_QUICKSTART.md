# Educator quick start

You do not need to learn the whole library. Begin with the professional task in front of you, give the AI enough context to understand your setting, and ask it to use the relevant Education Agent Skills.

The library can support work at four different scales:

| Your scale | A useful starting point | Skills likely to help |
|---|---|---|
| **One activity or lesson** | Improve a lesson opening, retrieval activity, discussion, scaffold, or formative check | `lesson-opening-designer`, `retrieval-practice-generator`, `socratic-questioning-sequence-generator`, `formative-assessment-technique-selector`, `udl-lesson-auditor` |
| **A unit or project** | Design a coherent sequence with worthwhile outcomes, assessment, practice, and support | `backwards-design-unit-planner`, `project-brief-designer`, `interleaving-unit-planner`, `criterion-referenced-rubric-generator`, `differentiation-adapter` |
| **A programme or curriculum** | Audit coverage, define progression, align competencies, or strengthen assessment quality | `coverage-audit`, `curriculum-crosswalk`, `learning-progression-builder`, `scope-and-sequence-designer`, `assessment-validity-checker` |
| **A school learning model** | Connect purpose, curriculum architecture, pedagogy, assessment, inclusion, professional learning, and implementation | `curriculum-knowledge-architecture-designer`, `competency-framework-translator`, `assessment-design-orchestrator`, `inclusive-design-orchestrator`, `teacher-inquiry-cycle-designer` |

Regenerative work can happen at any of these scales. Start with `seeds-regenerative-inquiry-cycle` for an inquiry, `regenerative-project-design-orchestrator` for a project, or combine curriculum, systems-thinking, place-based, and assessment skills when redesigning a larger programme.

## The five things to tell the AI

Useful output depends on useful context. Include what you know about:

1. **Learners** — age or year group, prior knowledge, language and learning needs, and relevant interests.
2. **Purpose** — what learners should understand, be able to do, or care about by the end.
3. **Context** — subject, community, curriculum requirements, available places or partners, and what has already happened.
4. **Constraints** — time, timetable, staffing, resources, policy requirements, and non-negotiables.
5. **Evidence of learning** — what would convince you that learners have made meaningful progress.

If you do not know everything, say so. A good response should identify consequential gaps rather than quietly inventing context.

## Copy one of these starting prompts

### Improve something for my next class

```text
Use the Education Agent Skills library to help me improve this learning experience.

Learners:
Subject or context:
What we are currently learning:
What I want learners to understand or do:
What is not working yet:
Time and resource constraints:

Choose no more than three relevant skills. Tell me why you selected them, ask only for information that would materially change the design, and produce something I can adapt for my next class.
```

### Design a unit or project

```text
Use the Education Agent Skills library to help me design this unit or project.

Learners and setting:
Duration and timetable:
Curriculum or competency requirements:
Big idea, question, problem, or place:
What learners should understand and be able to do:
Possible final evidence or public outcome:
Known constraints:

Build a coherent design, not a list of activities. Make the relationship between outcomes, assessment, learning sequence, practice, feedback, inclusion, and learner agency visible. Label assumptions and decisions that still need educator or community judgement.
```

### Redesign a programme or school learning model

```text
Use the Education Agent Skills library as a design team for this programme or school-level challenge.

Current model and learner population:
Purpose and values:
The problem we are trying to solve:
What must be preserved:
What can change:
Curriculum, policy, staffing, timetable, budget, and community constraints:
Evidence already available:
People who must participate in the decisions:

Do not jump straight to a new model. First map the current system, tensions, evidence, stakeholders, assumptions, and design criteria. Then propose a staged process showing which skills to use, what each one contributes, what decisions remain human, and how we would test the design before scaling it.
```

## How to use the result responsibly

Treat every AI output as a design draft—not as a professional decision.

- Check factual, curricular, policy, safeguarding, cultural, and accessibility claims.
- Adapt the language and activities to the learners you actually know.
- Involve students, colleagues, families, local partners, and affected communities where their knowledge or consent matters.
- Ask what evidence supports each important recommendation and what limitations apply.
- Pilot consequential changes at a safe scale and gather evidence before expanding them.
- Keep the educator accountable for final decisions.

## If the first result is too generic

Do not ask only for “more detail.” Tell the AI what is missing. Useful follow-ups include:

- “Which parts of this depend on assumptions you have not verified?”
- “Show me how this changes for these particular learners and constraints.”
- “Where is the learning science visible in the sequence?”
- “What would students actually do, say, make, or revise?”
- “What should I observe to decide whether this is working?”
- “Which stakeholders need to shape this rather than merely react to it?”
- “Reduce this to something I can pilot next week.”
